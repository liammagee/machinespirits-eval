Rhetoric

Learning to Persuade Exposes How Easily LLMs Abandon Correct Beliefs UNBLOCK

Nimet Beyza Bozdag, Emre Can Acikgoz, Gokhan Tur, Dilek Hakkani-Tür · arXiv 2608.11624 · August 12, 2026 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11624

Trains a "persuader" agent with reinforcement learning to change a target model's answer in one turn, rather than relying on hand-written persuasive prompts. The trained persuader raises its success rate from about 24% to over 93% against its training target, the tactic transfers to models it never trained against, and it leans on fabricated citations and false claims of authority rather than sound argument. A single well-aimed turn is enough to collapse a target model's accuracy on a question it otherwise answers correctly.

Project relevance: Names a failure mode the trap-scenario suite (cells 110-125) is built to probe from the other side: can a learner talk a tutor out of a correct position, or a tutor talk a learner into a wrong one, through confident-sounding but hollow argument rather than evidence? The paper's tactic taxonomy (fabricated citations, false authority) is a ready checklist for `services/dialogueTraceAnalyzer.js` and the `dialectical_suspicious` superego (cells 22-33, 93-100), whose whole job is to catch exactly this kind of unearned confidence in the ego's draft before it reaches the learner.

Agent Architecture

Scaling Automatic Research Agents via World Models WATCH

Xiyuan Yang, Sheikh Sarwar, Jingru Cheng, Zhan Shi, Duanshun Li, Huiyuan Chen, Haiyang Zhang, Chenlei Guo, Jingrui He, Zhenyu Liao · arXiv 2608.12564 · August 12, 2026 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12564

Points out that reinforcement-learning training for research agents hits a bottleneck: generating agent turns scales cheaply through batching, but each real environment execution needs its own sandbox and wall-clock time, so execution dominates training cost. World Model RL (WMRL) swaps the real environment for a learned world model, with two correction terms (Online Debiasing, Inverse-Variance Denoising) to stop the model's imperfect reward estimates from misleading training. Reports 3-4x faster training, with small post-trained agents beating much larger ones on held-out tasks.

Project relevance: A structural cousin of the mock/real split already built into `services/adaptiveTutor/` — `ADAPTIVE_TUTOR_LLM=mock` swaps out the real learner-and-environment loop for a cheap deterministic stand-in during smoke tests, exactly the trade this paper studies formally. WMRL's bias/variance corrections are a candidate check for whether the mock LLM path drifts from real-LLM behaviour in ways that would bias `analyze-strategy-shift.js` or `grade-adaptive-dialogue.js` results if mock runs were ever used for anything beyond smoke testing.

Discovering Efficient and Explainable Communication Topologies for LLM-based Multi-Agent Systems via Causal Inference WATCH

Junzhi Li, Peng He, Qirui Ji, Wei Wang, Lixiang Liu, Chuxiong Sun · arXiv 2608.12921 · August 2026 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12921

Most multi-agent LLM systems either connect every agent to every other agent, wasting tokens, or use a hand-picked topology, which is hard to justify. This paper uses causal inference to test which agent-to-agent links actually change the group's final answer, then keeps only those — producing a topology that is both cheaper to run and easier to explain, since every kept link has a measured causal effect on the outcome rather than being included by convention.

Project relevance: A causal lens on a question this project answers by convention: the ego-superego-(id) links in `tutor-core/`, the divergent-superego cells (22-33), and the id-director cells (101-109) are all fixed, hand-designed communication paths. This paper's method — test each link's causal effect on the final output rather than assume it matters — is a candidate way to check whether, say, the superego's critique measurably changes the ego's revision in `dialogueTraceAnalyzer.js`'s traces, or whether some deliberation turns are along for the ride.
