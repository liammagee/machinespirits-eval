How to build the ideal AI tutor

A recipe: the shortest evidence-backed path from first principles to a working adaptive tutor. Every step points at the paper section that licenses it, the arc file that carries the working detail, and the atlas module that tells the story plainly — instead of restating them.

The path, in one paragraph. Write the tutor's system prompt around an intersubjective stance — meet what this learner actually said — and stop at a three-part kernel, because stacking validated mechanisms adds nothing. Author the knowledge as a world file with a per-turn release schedule, and hand the tutor only the facts it cannot see. Cast both seats deliberately: a learner with a position to lose and states authored in per turn; a tutor model checked for the moves you need. Make adaptation criterial — a deterministic trigger, a typed move card at the detected moment, a licence where your own rules suppress the move — never advisory. Disclose state as legal moves, freshly computed, not as a scoreboard. Measure on judge-free outcome channels first, and tell the judge what the moment demanded before asking it to score. The rest of this page is those eight steps with their references.

01·

Steps 1–3 · the kernel

One prompt, three parts, and a critic only where the model needs it.

1

Write the stance into the system prompt supported

Do. Copy the intersubjective-pedagogy prompt — treat the learner as a thinking subject; engage what this learner said before responding; find what is valid in a wrong interpretation; pose a question when the learner is confused but engaged; name your own misstep on rupture. The verbatim text is Appendix A of the paper and prompts/tutor-ego-recognition.md. Ground it in any constructivist tradition you can write concretely (the traditions are interchangeable); never in drill/transmission framing, which scores below a generic baseline. Do not mandate empathy markers — within any condition, surface markers correlate negatively with quality.

Anchor. The largest reliable effect in the programme: judge-pooled d ≈ 1.63, unanimous across three models and three judges, and it works by lifting the floor, not the ceiling.

Read. §6.1 (the effect and its shape) · §8.3 (the pooled estimate and its judge band) · §7.9 (orientation, not vocabulary) · §7.10 (substance over costume) · Appendix A (prompt text) · atlas recognition-calibration · cells 5–8 in config/tutor-agents.yaml.

2

Stop at the three-part kernel supported

Do. Build exactly: the stance (step 1) + within-dialogue memory + register routing. Add further machinery only against a demonstrated, priced failure. The pre-registered composition test (cells 199–200) found the full mechanism stack never beats this kernel on any measured surface; the design rule is that repeated instructions converge on competence the model already has, and only new machine-checkable signal accumulates.

Read. §6.14 (the composition gate) · §7.11 (why sub-additive; the cue-and-right codicil) · pre-registration notes/2026-07-02-blueprint-composition-plan.md · module registry config/tutor-blueprint.yaml · atlas composition-small-kernel · runnable arms cell_199_blueprint_kernel_verified / cell_200_blueprint_full_verified.

3

Size the critic and the scaffolding to the model model-dependent

Do. On a weak or cheap model, add the critic loop — ego drafts, superego reviews reject-by-default, ego revises with final authority — and heavier step-by-step scaffolding. On a capable model, ship the stance single-agent and keep prompts light: the critic's benefit collapses once the stance is in (base deltas +9 to +27 points across the tested models; near zero under the stance on strong models, +12.3 only on the weakest), and heavy prompts tuned on a weak model actively harm a strong one.

Read. §6.2 (what the critic catches) · §6.4 (universal substitution) · §6.6, §8.3 (model dependence and its limits) · §7.8 (scaffolding inverted-U; destructive transfer) · atlas superego-error-correction, model-dependence · loop code tutorDialogueEngine.js, mirrored in learnerTutorInteractionEngine.js.

fast path Steps 1–2 alone give you the validated tutor. Steps 4–8 are what make it adaptive and measurable.

02·

Step 4 · the world

Author the knowledge as a world; hand facts over per turn.

4

Put the material in a world file with a release schedule and a due line development-tier

Do. Write what is to be taught as a world: facts, their dependency structure, and a per-turn schedule of what opens when. Each turn, hand the tutor a due line — the newly opened finding, release decision left to the speaker, nothing on quiet turns. Hand over only what the model cannot see; everything inferable from the visible transcript it already infers. And leave the refutation of the learner's fallback off the schedule — if the schedule answers every objection on time, you are measuring the schedule, not the tutor.

Anchor. Instrumentation pricing found exactly one advisory block pays on a plan-blind reading, and one sentence of hidden state carries its whole effect; in the misconception pair, moving one clause of one clue changed the close rate more than any tutoring behaviour observed in the arc.

Read. §6.23 (the pricing and the due-line control) · §6.24 (the misconception pair) · arc HOW-TO-BUILD-A-TUTOR.md §1 · harness doc docs/tutor-instrumentation-ab.md · authoring guide docs/tutor-stub-prompt-and-world-authoring.md · example pair world-032/033-alder-row.

the law §6.24 states the arc's one law: information placement and timing — the learner's state, the schedule's time — spent on whichever role needs it, at the turn it applies. The model plays; the harness keeps time.

03·

Step 5 · the cast and the harness

Cast both seats deliberately; keep the harness a timekeeper, never a co-author.

5

Cast the learner with stakes, the tutor for its moves, and check what actually ships development-tier

Do. Four rules, one per lock. (a) Learner: a one-paragraph character brief with a position to lose, a vernacular, and concessions that cost — then author affective states in per turn via a stress schedule (typed state, in-fiction cause, verbatim directive, adjudicated right repair); never try to read states out of transcripts, there is nothing there to tag. (b) Tutor: a model's repertoire is a property you test for, not a target you prompt toward — probe the family for the moves your bench needs before building mechanism around their absence. (c) Guards: binary for contracts (evidence leaks, clue bookkeeping, closure), advisory for style and progression; never enforce character at the mouth, and never let the fallback composer answer the pressured turns — that is where adaptation lives or dies. (d) Delivery: verify every per-turn instruction in the prompt that actually shipped, retries included — the arc's hardest "family boundary" dissolved when a retry path was found silently dropping the conduct card.

Read. §6.24 (the four locks; Phases S/H/L) · arc HOW-TO-BUILD-A-TUTOR.md §§2–4 and "The wall comes down" · guard catalog docs/tutor-stub-guard-catalog.md · stress-schedule gold world-033-stress-schedule.yaml · atlas ontology-state-interior (why richer learner models fail), advice-versus-enforcement.

two natures The harness's timekeeping half (schedule, due line, pacing, closure reader, leak guard) is the entire value; its enforcement half (costume vetoes, progression vetoes, the composer's mouth) subtracted value everywhere measured — HOW-TO-BUILD-A-TUTOR.md, "Is the harness worth having?".

04·

Steps 6–7 · adaptation

Hear the state, hand the move, at the turn — and disclose state as legal moves.

6

Make adaptation criterial: trigger + typed card + licence development-tier

Do. Do not ask the tutor to "be adaptive," and do not bet on it warming up across a session — within-session improvement is a clean, well-powered null (all slopes d ≤ 0.15, powered for 0.27); advisory steering makes conduct worse. Instead: (a) a deterministic trigger classifies the learner's turn (pressure states by pattern + cascade classifier; quiet states — boredom, confusion, quiet defiance — by their own typed detector, since they carry no pressure markers); (b) at a detected moment, inject a typed move card naming the move that state calls for, per turn, permission-shaped; (c) where a standing safety rule suppresses a needed move, carve the exception into the rule itself as a licence — cue and right are separately zero and work only paired. Timing and typing are separately necessary: a typed card at a detected moment works; an untyped card at the right moment fails; a typed card at the wrong moment hurts.

Anchor. The pre-registered claim gate passed on move cards (right-repairs 15/29 vs the never-adapting baseline's 10/24) after failing on temperament cards; replicated across a second world, persona, tagger family, and tutor family; the crossed experiment then showed state-matched moves are causal for conduct (right card 6/6 and 5/6 where the wrong card manufactures the characteristic error).

Read. §6.3 (the slope null — front-load turn 1) · §6.24 (claim gate, Phases Q/R, the crossed experiment) · §7.11 (cue-and-right codicil) · §7.12 (the consolidated boundary map: advice fails, structure works) · arc HOW-TO-BUILD-A-TUTOR.md (the playbook, the admission rule) · plan ADAPTATION-PLAN-3.0.md · code tutorStubMannerSwitch.js, tutorStubQuietDetector.js · atlas adaptive-null, adaptation-four-locks.

7

Disclose state action-shaped and freshly conditioned development-tier

Do. When the harness owns ground truth the tutor cannot see, disclose it as the legality projection — what may I do right now — recomputed at every read. Never a raw scoreboard (it loses to playing blind: the model's own re-derivation wins the argument against a state dump), and never persisted advice (state-shaped memory gets no uptake; action-shaped memory gets uptake including when stale, which is worse).

Read. §6.15 (the adversarial-game result: format, not information, is the bottleneck) · pre-registration and results AGON-GAME-PLAN.md · referee and arms services/agon/ · the same boundary from the speaking seat: §6.23's due line.

§7.12 The one-page version of where adaptation can live: every rung that delivers information fails to change behaviour; deterministic triggers + non-inferable signal + structural delivery is the shape of everything that survived.

05·

Step 8 · measurement

Measure on channels the defaults cannot win.

8

Judge-free outcomes first; disclose the moment to any judge you keep bounded

Do. (a) Score outcomes deterministically where possible: machine-checked closure, keyed demonstrations, planted-repair hits — and expect saturation with a diligent simulated learner (a bare frontier tutor closed 19/19 solvable worlds), so put outcome variance in the learner. (b) Keep a rubric panel for quality, but multi-judge, cross-family, per-judge reported — and know its construction: turn-local rubrics score a reply as a timeless artifact and structurally under-measure adaptation. One added sentence naming the learner's state roughly doubles judge–gold agreement; use rubric scores to detect template mass, never to compare manners. (c) Log symmetric per-step traces on both seats so every mechanism claim can be traced, and pin provenance hashes.

Read. §5.2 (rubric v2.2/v3.0), §4.3–§4.5 (traces, provenance, scoring) · §6.24 (lock four + the disclosure result) · §6.23 (the outcome-saturation pilot) · §8.2 (judge limits) · arc HOW-TO-BUILD-A-TUTOR.md §§5–5a · atlas rubrics-as-instruments, provable-discourse · pipeline node scripts/eval-cli.js evaluate <runId> with evaluation-rubric.yaml.

three self-preferences The judge prefers its family's prose; guards prefer their calibration family's shapes; the model prefers its trained role. Control all three or the bench reads the uncontrolled two as quality — HOW-TO-BUILD-A-TUTOR.md §5.

06·

The skip list

Documented nulls and backfires — spend the budget elsewhere.

  • Multi-layer episodic memory ("Writing Pad") — controlled ablation: not load-bearing; the stance lives in the prompt. §6.6.9
  • Learner-profiling prosthesis — six models: neutral once, harmful five times; capability does not predict benefit. §6.6.10
  • Theory-of-mind modules and richer learner-state schemas — no discrimination added on a capable model; richer sometimes worse. §6.8.5–.6 · §6.10
  • Features whose value is the tutor visibly "learning the student" within a session — the slope null; get turn 1 right. §6.3
  • Mandated empathy/paraphrase markers — more markers, worse answers, within every condition. §6.1.8 · §7.10
  • Stacking prompt + critic on a capable model and budgeting for addition — they substitute; the kernel result closes it. §6.4 · §6.14
  • Standing advice in any wrapper — persisted playbooks, coached consolidation into memory, point-of-action side-coaching: consumed wrongly, or not at all. §6.15 · §6.16 · §7.12
  • Profile-keyed register/style selection and uncued character shifts — no confirmed profile-contingent benefit; an unoccasioned costume prices negative twice. §6.17 · §6.23
  • A better hidden-state sensor — on the tested substrate the "hidden" state was recoverable from public history with zero uncertainty; build a genuinely ambiguous substrate first. §6.19
  • Enforcement at the mouth — costume vetoes and progression vetoes erase character under load and answer the learner's hardest turns with templates; enforcement buys typed transactions, never quality. §6.24 · §6.18
  • Product logic reading "genuinely reasoned" vs "complied" off text — unannotatable (κ = 0.084); two careful annotators read the same revisions oppositely. §6.12

The general form of most of these: re-derivation instruments die — anything that tells the model what the visible transcript already shows. See the condensed graveyard in HOW-TO-BUILD-A-TUTOR.md §6 and the atlas modules ontology-state-interior, advice-versus-enforcement, transparent-sensor-boundary.

read me as saved budgets. None is "impossible"; each is "shown not to carry the gain" on this evidence.

07·

What stays open

The unknowns a builder must keep in view.

  • Human learning. Everything above is simulated-learner evidence. The human pilot is built and waits on approval and materials. §8.1 · atlas human-learning-translation · routes/pilotRoutes.js
  • Conduct is causal; learning is not yet in the tutor's hands. State-matched moves cause better conduct, and taught learners do carry a method to close analogues — but no move-level lever for that transfer survived its causal test, and analogy distance bounds it. §6.24 (crossed experiment)
  • Outcome contrasts need a fallible learner. Diligent simulated learners saturate solvable worlds; the registered fallible-learner design holds that door. §6.23 · board card tutor-fallible-learner-closure-prereg
  • Model transience and thresholds. Results are pinned to named versions; the capability lines (critic worth keeping, scaffolding flip, refusal floor) are bracketed, not located — re-run the calibration checks on any new model. §8.3 · §7.8.3
  • Cost. The observability architecture is expensive (a 10-turn multi-agent dialogue ≈ 225k input tokens); the deployment break-even is undetermined. §8.7
  • Form vs learning. Whether a judge that rewards the look of good tutoring tracks learning at all awaits human expert coding. §8.2
