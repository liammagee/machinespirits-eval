From a null trajectory effect to a working control loop

In May, recognition did not steepen tutor trajectories relative to baseline. Today, a heavier controller can detect a particular learner state, choose a matching move, and make the delivered tutor reply fit that state. That is real progress — and a much narrower claim.

d = 0.03May: recognition did not change tutor trajectory slopes across N = 432 dialogues; every tested factor stayed at d ≤ 0.15.
5/6 vs 0/6Now: at the endgame stake, the matched card produced the target tutor conduct five times in six; the deliberately wrong card did so zero times in six.

The apparent reversal disappears once we ask what each result measures. The May study tested whether lightweight prompts and an inner critic changed a diffuse conversation-level slope. The later work built a control system around one local decision: what kind of stuckness is this, and what should the tutor do next? The advance is not “the old hypothesis was right.” It is that adaptation became explicit enough to test causally.

01·

The architectural story

Four changes turned “be adaptive” into an observable decision.

The important shift was not adding ever more advice. It was separating the jobs that had previously been folded into one fluent reply.

01 · MAY

Prompt + inner critic

The tutor drafts, critiques, and revises. Both baseline and recognition tutors adapt descriptively, but the experimental factors do not change the trajectory.

Null on modulation
02 · EXTERNAL STATE

Make the lesson inspectable

A proof DAG records what follows from which clues; a release schedule records what the learner is allowed to know at each turn.

Premature release becomes detectable
03 · TYPED MOVES

Name the stuck state

Demand, mockery, grievance, a false memory, and a fused social stake become different states with different candidate actions.

State → action can be crossed
04 · CLOSED LOOP

Measure what shipped and what followed

The system records sensing, selected card, delivered text, and later outcomes separately. A wrong card can now be forced as a causal control on tutor conduct.

Architecture lessonInstrumentation has at least four jobs: represent visible state, inject unavailable state, choose a move, and verify delivery. The results improve only when the job and the missing causal link match.

07·

Device 4 · delivery and outcome ledger

A treatment cannot explain an outcome if the harness rewrote it before delivery.

One of the programme's most useful corrections was methodological. Under the strict guard, a deterministic safety template replaced the tutor's drafted wording on 62% of turns. The first trial looked null partly because both arms were often receiving the guard's prose. Flipping the guard to advisory let the planned wording arrive — and exposed a negative effect on the registered legitimate-closure endpoint.

Strict guardTemplate authored 62% of turns. Contract and bare appeared level: 22/33 vs 20/29 legitimate closures, p = 1.000.
Advisory guardThe first draft shipped unaltered on 90–96% of contract turns. The contract then reduced legitimate closure: 12/36 vs 24/36, p = 0.0091.

The architecture improved the honesty of the experiment before it improved the headline.Paper 2.0 §6.23–6.24 · simulated learners

The mechanism was visible in the transcript form: contract tutors asked many more late yes/no questions, often supplying the candidate conclusion inside the question. Learners could answer without ever carrying the conclusion themselves — exactly what the closure endpoint required. This is not a success story disguised as a failure. It is the control loop doing its scientific job: showing that a delivered device can matter in the wrong direction.

Limit This negative contract result is a separate registered trial. It explains why delivery must be measured; it is not part of the crossed-card 5/6 vs 0/6 estimate.

→

The clearest present claim supported, bounded

For these simulated learners, worlds, and tutor stack, forcing the action matched to a local learner state can cause the delivered tutor reply to exhibit better state-appropriate conduct than forcing a plausible mismatched action. The apparatus now records enough of that chain to say why. It does not yet show a move-level transfer benefit or human learning.
