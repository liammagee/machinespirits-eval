P1 · Two-level meta-rubrics as a completeness check on the v2.2 and poetics judge prompts — "Two-Level Meta-Rubrics for Evaluating Open-Ended Generation: GAMUT" (2607.19322)

Why: GAMUT's point is that factuality grading has focused on precision — are the claims correct — and skipped completeness — does the response cover everything it should, where the "should-cover" set is not a flat checklist but has order and relationships between facts. The current judge prompts for content_accuracy (config/evaluation-rubric.yaml:268) and the poetics dimensions anagnorisis / cathartic_closure (config/evaluation-rubric-poetics.yaml) are single-shot Likert prompts asking a judge to grade exactly this kind of coverage-and-order criterion in one pass — the failure mode GAMUT names directly.
Do: hand-write a GAMUT-style two-level check for one dimension on each rubric — a short checklist of what a complete content_accuracy turn or a resolved anagnorisis should contain, drawn from the rubric's own written criteria — then hand-score (a person, not a judge model) a small existing sample of already-generated, already-judged turns against that checklist. Compare the hand-scored checklist result to the existing single-shot judge score on the same turns to see where they agree and where the checklist catches a coverage gap the Likert score missed.
Evaluate: a design pilot on already-generated dialogue turns and already-recorded scores (any cell scored under v2.2 for the rubric arm; poetics-scored dramas for the poetics arm) — hand-coding only, no new model calls. existing data / hand-coded pilot

P2 · Scaffolding-collapse signal for the dogmatic-protocol-control drama family — "Mitigating Scaffolding Collapse in Socratic Tutors via Representation Alignment" (2607.19371)

Why: the paper names and studies the exact failure this project's dogmatic_routine_control condition (services/curriculum/curriculumCompiler.js) is built to provoke: under sustained pressure to close out, a Socratic tutor drops the scaffold and just confirms the answer. The paper's diagnosis is that surface-text defenses miss the drift that precedes the collapse — which matters here because the poetics rubric currently scores collapse only after the fact, from the finished transcript, the same way a surface-text defense would.
Do: read-only audit. Pull the existing poetics-scored dramas run under dogmatic_routine_control (and the related mismatched_rhetorical_dramatic_plan condition), hand-code each transcript's last two tutor turns for a plain binary — did the tutor confirm/sign off the answer, yes or no — and check that against the recorded anagnorisis and cathartic_closure scores from config/evaluation-rubric-poetics.yaml. The question is whether the rubric already catches every hand-coded collapse, or whether some collapses score fine because the rubric grades the whole arc rather than the specific late-turn giveaway.
Evaluate: existing poetics-ingested drama rows (npm run poetics:report / the poetics browse UI) for the two conditions above — no new drama generation, no new scoring. existing data

P3 · A three-dimension lens on superego-incorporation for the divergent-superego cells — "Beyond Sycophancy: Structured Resistance and Compliance in LLM Moral Reasoning" (2607.21558)

Why: the paper finds a model's willingness to revise a judgment under pressure tracks three things — how far the pushback sits from its own position, who the pushback is attributed to, and whether it reads as one voice or a group. Cells 22-33 (config/tutor-agents.yaml) manipulate exactly this kind of pressure by register — suspicious, adversary, advocate, each in base and recognition and each in a plain and dialectical form — but services/dialogueTraceAnalyzer.js's analyzeSuperegoIncorporation() currently reduces every ego revision to one number, incorporationRate.
Do: read-only recoding. Take existing tutor_deliberation_* logs for cells 22-33 and hand-code a sample of ego revisions against the paper's three dimensions — was the superego critique close to the ego's own prior stance, was it framed as the ego's own reasoning reflected back, did the adversary/advocate register function like a group-pressure manipulation — then check whether cells where the ego resists more (lower incorporationRate) also score differently on those three dimensions, or whether the single incorporation number is hiding the same three-way structure the paper describes.
Evaluate: existing tutor_deliberation_*, incorporation_rate and dimension_convergence rows for cells 22-33 — an npm run analyze:traces re-analysis, no new generation. existing data
