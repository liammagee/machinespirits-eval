## 2. What the frozen text settles

The registration's purpose clause ("holding the words fixed isolates
per-turn hint selection and timing as the contrast") settles two
points prospectively:

1. **No partial paste.** A binding that holds fewer words than the
   gate can ever inject is ruled out. If the standing-permission
   tutor lacks words the gated tutor can receive, a Gated-over-
   standing result cannot separate machinery from missing words, and
   the §5 reading rule collapses.
2. **No flat concatenation.** Pasting mutually exclusive branch
   instructions as one block gives the standing tutor contradictory
   orders. That builds the machinery's advantage into the condition —
   a strawman — and is also ruled out.

## 3. What the frozen text does not settle

Two choices remain free after §2, and each can move the measured
outcome:

1. **Scope of the union.** Which strings count as "the gate's" text:
   the catalogue description, the first-draft cue, the stance/part/
   tactic instructions picked after the gate decision, and the
   question-support branches — the boundary between the gate's words
   and the stub's words is a judgment the registration never made.
2. **The conditional prefixes.** The only coherent full paste is a
   menu: each branch's verbatim string under a plain statement of
   when the gate would inject it. Those condition statements are NEW
   words in the tutor's standing instructions, written by whoever
   binds this, and their phrasing can shift tutor conduct — the very
   thing measured. A third reading (reconstruct the Phase-5 smoke
   prompt) tests a different surface again.

Choosing among readings that the frozen text does not settle is a
design decision. Registration §7 and note 052e reserve design
decisions for the human. **The reviewer does not bind this
unattended.**
